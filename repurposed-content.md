## LinkedIn Post

I've been learning wheel-thrown pottery for two years now, and this Blue Teapot is one of the pieces I'm proudest of.

It's made from Buño clay, from a village in Galicia, Spain that has been dedicated to pottery for centuries. The blue glaze on the outside and white porcelain interior were inspired by Fauvist paintings — bright colors, clean straight lines. Each piece takes about a month from start to finish: thrown on the wheel, bisque-fired to harden it, glazed, then fired a second time to make it durable enough for daily use.

It's fully functional: heat resistant, microwave and dishwasher safe (just don't put it over an open flame). And because it's shaped entirely by hand, no two pieces are ever exactly alike.

There's something I keep coming back to with this craft: you start with a shapeless lump of clay, and slowly, with practice, it becomes something useful and beautiful. My family has its own history with pottery — my mother grew up with large jars and pots from my grandmother's village — and I like to think I'm continuing that thread in my own small way, one piece at a time.
