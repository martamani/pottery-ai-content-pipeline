## Editing Pass — Full Example

The AI draft was accurate but slightly overwritten for this voice — a few redundant adverbs, one technical-sounding word choice, one comma splice. None of these are "errors" a spellchecker would catch; they're tone judgment calls, edited against the source voice (see Editorial Guidelines below).

| Before (AI draft) | After (edited) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "this Blue Teapot" | "the blue teapot" | Mid-sentence, capitalizing the product name like a proper noun reads like a brand drop-in, not a personal story. |
| "from a village in Galicia, Spain that has been dedicated..." | "from a village in Galicia, Spain, that has been dedicated..." | Missing comma before a non-restrictive clause. |
| "outside and white porcelain interior were inspired" | "outside and white porcelain on the inside are inspired" | "Outside" vs. "interior" is a mismatched pair — "outside" should oppose "inside." Also switched "were" to "are": no reason for past tense here. |
| "durable enough for daily use" | "hard enough for daily use" | "Durable" reads like a technical spec sheet. The source voice is plain and handmade, not a product listing. |
| "no two pieces are ever exactly alike" | "no two pieces are the same" | "Ever exactly" is redundant — two adverbs doing one job. The craft is simple and a little raw; the sentence should be too. |
| "continuing that thread in my own small way" | "continuing that thread in my own way" | Same redundancy pattern — cut the extra qualifier. |
| "made for actual daily use" | "made for daily use" | "Actual" adds nothing here. |

**The underlying principle:** the source text is written in first person, plainly, and undersells itself — the maker says she came to pottery *by chance*, with *no special calling, just curiosity*. That modesty is the voice, not a gap to fill with confident marketing language. AI drafts tend to add adverbs and technical-sounding polish by default; editing here means cutting that back toward plainness.

## LinkedIn Post

I've been learning wheel-thrown pottery for two years now, and the blue teapot is one of the pieces I'm proudest of.

It's made from Buño clay, from a village in Galicia, Spain that has been dedicated to pottery for centuries. The blue glaze on the outside and white porcelain interior were inspired by Fauvist paintings — bright colors, clean straight lines. Each piece takes about a month from start to finish: thrown on the wheel, bisque-fired to harden it, glazed, then fired a second time to make it durable enough for daily use.

It's fully functional: heat resistant, microwave and dishwasher safe (just don't put it over an open flame). And because it's shaped entirely by hand, no two pieces are ever exactly alike.

There's something I keep coming back to with this craft: you start with a shapeless lump of clay, and slowly, with practice, it becomes something useful and beautiful. My family has its own history with pottery — my mother grew up with large jars and pots from my grandmother's village — and I like to think I'm continuing that thread in my own small way, one piece at a time.
