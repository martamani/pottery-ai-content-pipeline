# Frequently Asked Questions

**What is wheel-thrown pottery?**
Wheel-thrown pottery is ceramic work shaped by hand on a spinning potter's wheel, rather than cast from a mold. The process starts with the clay being wedged, shaped into a ball, and placed on the wheel, then centered, thrown up and down, and opened up before being shaped by hand and refined with a bamboo half-round molding tool. Because each piece is shaped individually, no two are exactly alike.

**How long does it take to make a piece of pottery?**
Production time is approximately one month per piece. This covers throwing, a first firing to harden the clay (bisquing), glazing or coating in porcelain, and a second firing to make the piece durable and food-safe.

**Why does each piece look slightly different?**
No two pieces are exactly alike, since each one is shaped by hand and not made from molds. Small variations in shape and glaze are part of handmade wheel-thrown work, not a flaw.

**What is bisque firing, and why does pottery need to be fired twice?**
Once thrown, a piece is bisque-fired to harden it, then glazed or coated in porcelain, which makes it usable, prevents water from seeping through, and ensures it's non-toxic. The bisque-fired piece is fragile and breaks easily, so it has to go back into the kiln a second time to make it durable.

**Is this pottery earthenware?**
No. This is not earthenware — even though the pieces can be used every day, they break more easily than earthenware. Treat handmade stoneware/porcelain-glazed pieces with a bit more care than mass-produced dishware.

**Is the pottery safe to use for food and drink?**
Yes. Every piece is glazed or coated in porcelain, which makes it usable, prevents water from seeping through, and ensures it's non-toxic. All current pieces (the Blue Teapot, Blue Cunca, and White Cunquelo) are listed as heat resistant and safe for microwave and dishwasher use.

**Can I put this pottery over an open flame or in the oven?**
No. The pieces are heat resistant and microwave/dishwasher safe, but should not be placed directly over an open flame.

**What clay and glazes are used?**
The pieces are made with Buño clay — from Buño, a village in Galicia, northern Spain, that has centuries of pottery-making tradition — combined with porcelain and Mayco glazes (FN-006 blue, FN-001 white), depending on the piece.

**What is the tradition behind this pottery style?**
The pieces follow the pottery tradition of Buño, Galicia, Spain, a village with centuries of ceramic craft. The maker's own family history includes large traditional jars and pots from Gundivós, another Galician village, passed down from her grandmother's house.

**What pieces are currently available?**
Four pieces are documented so far: the Blue Teapot (Buño clay with porcelain interior, blue exterior glaze, 24 cm high x 18 cm wide, 1,050 g, Fauvist-inspired design); the Blue Cunca bowl (based on the traditional cunca broth-bowl shape, carved design inspired by seaweed, 8 cm high x 13 cm wide, 320 g); the White Cunquelo small cup (based on the traditional handleless wine cup, 7 cm high x 9 cm wide, 180 g); and the Blue Cup and Saucer Set (Buño clay with blue and white glaze).

**How do I clean and care for the pottery?**
The glazed pieces are dishwasher and microwave safe. Since the pieces are not earthenware, avoid sudden temperature shocks such as open flame, and handle them with the same care you would any handmade ceramic.
