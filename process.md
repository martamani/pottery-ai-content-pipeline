# Step-by-Step Process

First, the clay is wedged, shaped into a ball, and placed on the wheel. You center it, throw it up and down, and make a hole in the middle. Then you open it up, shape it with your hands, and finally refine it using a piece of bamboo half-round molding.

Once finished, it goes into the kiln for bisquing — a first firing to harden it. Next, it is glazed or coated in porcelain. This makes it usable, prevents water from seeping through, and ensures it's non-toxic.

A bisque-fired piece is fragile and breaks easily, so it has to go back into the kiln to make it durable, and then it's ready. Keep in mind that this is not earthenware — even though you can use it every day, it breaks more easily.

The production time is approximately one month. No two pieces are exactly alike, since each one is shaped by hand and not made from molds.
