# Editorial Guidelines — Marta's Pottery

A short reference for keeping AI-assisted content consistent with the source voice.

## The voice

Written in first person, plainly. The maker undersells rather than oversells: she came to pottery *by chance*, with *no special calling, just curiosity*. That modesty is the voice — not a gap to fill in with confident marketing language.

The tone draws on Richard Sennett's *The Craftsman*, which frames craft as a humbling discipline rather than a talent or a brand. The writing should reflect that: quiet, specific, unhurried, a little raw — not polished into a product pitch.

## Rules

1. **Cut redundant adverbs.** "Ever exactly," "actual daily," "in my own small way" — say it once, plainly. Craft pieces are simple and a little raw; the sentence should be too.
2. **Avoid technical/spec-sheet language in narrative copy.** "Durable" belongs on a product spec line, not in a personal story — use plainer words like "hard enough" or "sturdy."
3. **Match register to context.** Product names ("Blue Teapot") can be capitalized on product pages, but read like a brand drop-in in casual first-person prose — lowercase them there ("the blue teapot").
4. **Keep opposing pairs parallel.** "Outside" pairs with "inside," not "interior" — small mismatches like this read as slightly off, even if grammatically fine.
5. **Present tense for facts that are still true.** Don't default to past tense ("were inspired by") when describing something still true today ("are inspired by").
6. **Preserve the undersell.** Don't let AI drafts upgrade modesty into confidence — "I was just curious" should stay "just curious," not become "driven by a passion for the craft."

## Quick check before publishing

- Does it sound like something the maker would actually say out loud?
- Have any adverbs snuck in that don't earn their place?
- Does anything sound like a product description instead of a personal account?
