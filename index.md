# Marta's Pottery

My name is Marta. I've been learning wheel-thrown pottery for two years now. I'm in no rush. It takes a long time to master the technique and understand how the clay behaves — learning to tell when a piece is well-made, and watching how, with enough practice, the lines become finer and cleaner.

I stumbled into this by chance. I found a pottery studio right across from the beach in my city. I had seen pottery wheels before and tried one once during a trip. It's not that I have a special calling for it; I was just curious.

I'd say making pottery helps you appreciate the beauty of things a bit more. There's also something magical about watching a completely shapeless lump of clay transform into an object that can be useful, functional, and evocative.

My mother's house used to have very large pottery pieces — large jars and pots that came from my grandmother's village, inland in Galicia, in northern Spain. When she was a child, her neighbours would borrow oxcarts to bring the pieces from Gundivós, where they were made, to the market, where they were sold. She could hear the clattering of the pieces against each other all along the way.

My pieces are made following the tradition of Buño, another village in Galicia that has been dedicated to pottery for many, many centuries. I'm learning how to make traditional pieces — they have beautiful shapes — reworking them for today: Brighter colors, keeping what made them work in the first place. 
The essence remains. The surface changes.
