# Marta's Pottery

My name is Marta. I've been learning wheel-thrown pottery for two years now. I'm in no rush. It takes a long time to master the technique and understand how the clay behaves — even just learning to tell when a piece is well-made, and watching how, with enough practice, lines become finer and cleaner.

I stumbled into this by chance. One day I was walking along and found a studio right across from the beach in my city. I had seen pottery wheels in movies and tried one once during a trip. It's not that I have a special calling for it; I was just curious.

I had never done anything like this before, and I'd say it helps me appreciate the beauty of things a bit more. There is also something magical about it. You watch a shapeless lump of clay transform into an object that can be useful, beautiful, and evocative.

My mother's house used to have very large pottery pieces — large jars and pots that came from my grandmother's village, inland in Galicia, in northern Spain. My mother used to tell me that when she was a child, they would borrow oxcarts to bring the pieces from Gundivós, where they were made, and she could hear the clattering of the pieces against each other all along the way.

My pieces are made following the tradition of Buño, another village in Galicia that has been dedicated to pottery for many, many centuries. I'd love to learn how to make traditional pieces — they have beautiful shapes — and see them evolve with time. The essence remains, but the surface changes.
